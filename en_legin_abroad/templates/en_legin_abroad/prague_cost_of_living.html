03 / Know what’s included

A little context before you pack.

Housing

Start with your actual rent.

Rent is usually the biggest variable. The example rents below are editable planning assumptions. They do not represent market averages. Location, size, and condition matter.

Check whether an advert includes building charges, heating, and energy. Put each cost in your budget only once.

Getting around

One pass goes a long way.

The standard personal Prague adult pass costs 550 Kč monthly. A 3,650 Kč annual pass works out to about 304 Kč per month, but you pay upfront. The couple example includes two monthly passes.

These fares cover Prague zones P, 0 and B. Airport Express, travel outside Prague, and taxis need a separate budget. Check official PID fares ↗

Arriving & settling in

Keep a separate move-in fund.

This calculator covers recurring monthly spending. A rental deposit, any agency fee, moving, furniture, and initial travel are additional upfront costs. Ask for a complete breakdown before signing.

Use take-home income after payroll deductions. Add any separately paid health insurance, visa costs, debts, car costs, or dependants’ expenses to “Other & buffer.”

Compare two rental offers using the same budget

Suppose one person takes home 50,000 Kč and has 13,050 Kč in other monthly expenses. An apartment at 22,000 Kč plus 4,500 Kč in bills costs 26,500 Kč per month. An offer at 24,000 Kč with 2,000 Kč in bills costs 26,000 Kč. Enter the second offer's rent and utilities separately. With the other inputs held steady, the calculator shows 10,950 Kč left each month, 500 Kč more than the first offer. Before comparing, check that both offers include the same services and whether any charge is an advance that gets reconciled later.

Work out the cash you need before moving in

In the first rental example, the person spends 39,550 Kč in a normal month. If a lease also requires a 22,000 Kč deposit and you set aside 5,000 Kč for moving and basic furnishings, you need 66,550 Kč for that first month. The monthly total already includes the first rent payment. Keep one-time costs separate and replace the example figures with the amounts due before your first payday.

Method: add monthly expenses, subtract them from household take-home income, and multiply the balance by 12 for the annual projection. A negative balance is a shortfall.
